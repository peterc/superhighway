It's been another busy year for our favourite Gopher and to close out the year, we've taken a look at the data from the popular [Golang Weekly newsletter](https://golangweekly.com "Golang Newsletter").

The email newsletter is read by over 29,000 developers --- here's a look at what they clicked on in 2019:

# The Go highlights of 2019:

**[Practical Go: Real World Advice for Writing Maintainable Go Programs](https://dave.cheney.net/practical-go/presentations/qcon-china.html)**

Dave Cheney shared this thorough list of best-practice advice for writing Go code, covering indentifiers, package design, project structure, error handling and more. Good stuff.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Understanding Real-World Concurrency Bugs in Go](https://songlh.github.io/paper/go-study.pdf "Understanding Real-World Concurrency Bugs in Go")**

This report, characterized as the "first systematic study" of its kind, looks at popular Go applications (*think Docker, Kubernetes, etc.*), analyzing 171 concurrency bugs, their fixes, and if they are reproducible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[An Overview of Go's Tooling](https://www.alexedwards.net/blog/an-overview-of-go-tooling)**

Simply calling this an \"overview\" seemed a little insufficient. This well-received tome of information detailed how to use Go to do just about everything from linting to rewrite rules to stress testing. Bookmark this one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Go 1.14 Should Be Great](https://twitter.com/i/web/status/1189631604324327424)**

We're still waiting on Go 1.14, but it seems there is plenty of interest in the next major release ([a beta is available now](https://groups.google.com/forum/#!msg/golang-nuts/49xFw3IkrEw/yxSf1TG8AgAJ)). This [tweet thread](https://twitter.com/i/web/status/1189631604324327424) highlighed what to expect. A [popular slidedeck from Daniel Martí](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1HfIwlVTmVWQk94OLKfTGvXpQxyp0U4ywG1u5j2tjiuE/edit#slide=id.g550f852d27_228_0) also ran through some personal picks on what's looking good in the upcoming release.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Working with Errors in Go 1.13](https://blog.golang.org/go1.13-errors)**

Error handling is a common topic of [discussion](https://www.infoq.com/news/2019/07/go-try-proposal-rejected/) (*and, sometimes, [innovation](https://godoc.org/golang.org/x/xerrors)* ) and as such the improvements made in Go 1.13 (*new ways to examine, wrap, and unwrap errors*) proved popular.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Best Practices for Writing High-Performance Go Code](https://github.com/dgryski/go-perfbook)**

This document outlines best practices for writing optimized and performant Go code. It's been updated several times over the years and remains a popular resource. It's avaialble in English, Spanish and Chinese.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Go is on a Trajectory to Become the Next Enterprise Programming Language](https://hackernoon.com/go-is-on-a-trajectory-to-become-the-next-enterprise-programming-language-3b75d70544e)**

In this article from May, Kevin Goslar makes several worthwhile points about the characteristics that give Go an advantage in building large-scale software projects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[The Go Team Rejects the try Error Checking Function Proposal](https://github.com/golang/go/issues/32437#issuecomment-512035919)**

Back in July, following a lot of feedback from the wider community the Go Core Team declined a proposal for a `try` built-in Go error check function due to it \"*not targeting a worthwhile problem*\".

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[The Top 10 Most Common Mistakes I've Seen in Go Projects](https://itnext.io/the-top-10-most-common-mistakes-ive-seen-in-go-projects-4b79d4f6cd65)**

In this blog Teiva Harsanyi listed some of the most common mistakes he sees in Go projects, including problems with benchmarking and pointers, confusion using `break`, slice initialization, and variable shadowing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**[Quick Go Performance Improvements](https://stephen.sh/posts/quick-go-performance-improvements)**

A popular article with a handful of \"*techniques you can use to significantly improve the performance of your program with little effort*\".
